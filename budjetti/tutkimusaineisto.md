## Varzim SC (Póvoa de Varzim) — current sporting profile and finances, 2021-2026

- LEAGUE 2025-26: Varzim SC played in Liga 3 (Portuguese 3rd tier), Serie A, in 2025-26; they had the best attack of the group stage (26 goals in 18 games), qualified for the 8-team fase de subida (promotion phase), but finished 6th in it with 13 points as of 4 May 2026, while Amarante (25 pts) won the title and Amarante + Academica de Coimbra were promoted to Liga Portugal 2 (sources: zerozero.pt, Wikipedia 2025-26 Liga 3, Flashscore/Maisfutebol, diariocoimbra.pt).
- LEAGUE 2026-27: Varzim remain in Liga 3 for 2026-27 (their 5th consecutive 3rd-tier season); the season began 9 Aug 2026 and Varzim opened with wins 1-0 vs Trofense and 2-1 vs Marco 09 under new head coach Carlos Fernandes, appointed 24 June 2026 (sources: A Bola 16 Aug 2026, maissemanario.pt, bolanarede.pt).
- RECENT SEASONS: Varzim were relegated from Liga Portugal 2 at the end of 2021-22 (17th place) — NOT 2023 as commonly assumed; Liga 3 record since: 2022-23 5th in Serie A first phase (33 pts, missed promotion phase, safe in maintenance phase); 2023-24 6th of 8 in promotion stage; 2024-25 4th in promotion phase with 22 pts, one point behind 3rd-placed Belenenses and thus missing the II Liga playoff; 2025-26 6th in promotion phase (sources: Wikipedia Varzim S.C./season articles, SAPO Desporto, Maisfutebol).
- ACCOUNTS FY2023-24 (1 Jul 2023-30 Jun 2024): the club (Varzim SC) posted a positive result of ~EUR 440,000 while the football company Varzim SDUQ posted a loss of ~EUR 1,051,000; approved by ~70 members at the AGM; financial director Pedro Coelho said the SDUQ was 'in technical bankruptcy' and the model 'not viable' (sources: maissemanario.pt, radioondaviva.pt, vozdapovoa.com).
- ACCOUNTS FY2024-25: club profit of EUR 372,000 and SDUQ loss of EUR 820,000; approved unanimously by the 63 members present at the AGM (sources: maissemanario.pt, radioondaviva.com).
- BUDGET 2026-27: with the PER (special revitalisation process) closed, the board announced Varzim enters 2026-27 'free of liabilities', with a budget forecasting ~EUR 1.75 million in revenues against ~EUR 1.73 million in expenses (source: maissemanario.pt 'Varzim: Contas aprovadas por unanimidade e SAD so depois do PER resolvido', mid-2026).
- DEBT/PER: total liabilities of club + SDUQ were ~EUR 6.5 million; the PER plan foresaw roughly half the debt being forgiven; sócios approved sale of the training ground (campo de treinos, approved 1-2 Aug 2025) and of the old headquarters (EUR 663,000) to pay down debt and close the PER; PER ratification had been approved by members with court homologation still pending as of Sept 2024, and was closed by mid-2026 (sources: maissemanario.pt, caxinastv.wordpress.com 2 Aug 2025, O Jogo).
- EARLIER CLUB BUDGET BENCHMARK: the club-side budget approved in 2021 (season 2021-22) forecast revenues of ~EUR 746,000 and expenses of EUR 558,000 plus EUR 223,000 in payments under creditor agreements, approved by ~120 members (source: maissemanario.pt).
- STADIUM: Estadio do Varzim Sport Club (Povoa de Varzim), built 1929, capacity 7,280, natural grass 105x68m; condition is poor: the north stand was closed for all of 2021-22 for safety (new roof works started June 2022), the west (poente) stand was also interdicted after a Liga Portugal inspection, an estimated EUR 3.0 million is needed to rebuild the east (nascente) upper stand, and in February 2025 a stand section collapsed during away-fan celebrations, leading to temporary interdiction of the stadium (sources: pt.wikipedia.org, zerozero.pt, Record, O Jogo, A Bola 21 Feb 2025, maissemanario.pt).
- STADIUM/ASSETS FUNDING: members approved in March 2018 a project to requalify the current stadium and build a training centre in Parque da Cidade; a 2019 plan valued the club's Parque da Cidade land sale to the municipality at ~EUR 1.3 million (EUR 25/m2); in 2026 the Tribunal de Contas cleared the Camara Municipal da Povoa de Varzim to buy club land next to the stadium for EUR 2.4 million (sources: cm-pvarzim.pt, Record, maissemanario.pt).
- ATTENDANCE: Varzim drew 25,473 spectators over 9 home games in the Liga 3 first phase (average 2,830), the best in Liga 3 and 16th best in all of Portugal — higher than 5 Primeira Liga and 16 Liga 2 clubs; Liga 3 league-wide average in 2025-26 was ~2,271; Varzim-Academica in the 2025-26 promotion phase drew 5,498 (best single Liga 3 crowd), and the home opener vs Sp. Braga B drew over 4,000 (sources: maissemanario.pt, Record).
- MEMBERS: Varzim announced passing 5,000 sócios in September 2023 (a level previously reached in summer 2015); however only ~1,757 members had paid-up quotas in 2021, with a 2021-22 target of 2,600 paying members (sources: maissemanario.pt, vozdapovoa.com, Record).
- SQUAD 2026-27: 28 players, average age 25.3, 8 foreigners (28.6%) per Transfermarkt; head coach Carlos Fernandes; sporting director Andre Andre (ex-FC Porto midfielder, officialised 23 June 2026); coach churn in 2025-26: Alvaro Madureira (hired June 2025 on 1+1 contract, sacked before Christmas 2025 after a 0-1 home loss to Trofense) was replaced by Nuno Capucho (third spell, Dec 2025-May 2026, failed promotion and left); Vitor Paneira had left in May 2025 after three seasons (sources: transfermarkt.us, e24.pt, Record, O Jogo, maissemanario.pt).
- OWNERSHIP/STRUCTURE: professional football is run by Varzim Sport Club Futebol, SDUQ, Lda — a single-quota company 100% owned by the club (formed under Decree-Law 10/2013); there is NO SAD yet; a previous attempt to bring in an investor via SAD was rejected by members, causing the then-board (Edgar Pinho) to resign en masse and an administrative commission to run the club; Ricardo Nunes (former Varzim goalkeeper, 41) was elected president on 25 May 2024 with 228 of 247 votes for a four-year term (sources: fairplay.pt/rodaviva.pt, Record, A Bola 25 May 2024, DN, vozdapovoa.com).
- SAD PLANS: in 2026 the board consulted sócios (200+ present) on creating a SAD to strengthen the team and infrastructure; members signalled ambition but doubts about investor terms; leadership committed that a SAD vote happens only after the PER was resolved, with full disclosure of investors/amounts/governance, documents available 20 days in advance and a secret ballot (sources: maissemanario.pt 'Sem dividas e com ambicao: Varzim testa apoio dos socios a uma SAD', 'Varzim: Contas aprovadas por unanimidade e SAD so depois do PER resolvido').
- AMBITIONS: president Ricardo Nunes repeatedly stated the objective of returning Varzim to Liga Portugal 2 ('Partimos com a pujanca e a esperanca de atingir o que todos sonhamos', Record, before the 2026 promotion phase) while insisting on financial discipline: in April 2024 he called the existing budget 'unrealistic for the club's reality' (Renascenca) and in May 2024 said 'with me Varzim will not keep living beyond its means' (Record).
- HISTORY: Varzim have 21 seasons in the Portuguese top flight (22nd most top-flight games all-time); debut in 1963-64 (10th place); a 13-season top-flight run from the mid-1960s with a best finish of 6th (1969-70); last Primeira Liga season was 2002-03, when they went down with 36 points — at the time the highest points total ever for a relegated team (sources: pt.wikipedia.org Varzim Sport Club, maissemanario.pt 'O Varzim entre a elite nacional', conversasredondas.wordpress.com, Wikipedia 2002-03 Primeira Liga).
- YOUTH: Varzim SC (the club, not the SDUQ) runs all youth football up to U19 plus women's teams and other sports; the club has a strong 'clube formador' (development club) reputation and co-organises one of Portugal's largest youth tournaments covering U6-U14 (sources: varzim.pt, jpn.up.pt, cm-pvarzim.pt).

## Liga 3 (Portuguese third tier) — club budgets and economics, 2021/22–2025/26

- Format 2025/26 (5th edition, 'Liga 3 Placard'): 20 clubs in two series of 10 (Serie A north, Serie B south), double round-robin first phase; top 4 of each series go to an 8-team Promotion Phase of 14 matchdays; 1st and 2nd are promoted directly to Liga Portugal 2; 3rd plays a two-legged playoff against the 16th of Liga Portugal 2; the other 12 clubs play a Maintenance/Relegation phase in two series of 6 with the bottom 2 of each (4 clubs) relegated to Campeonato de Portugal (en.wikipedia.org 2025-26 Liga 3; ojogo.pt; fpf.pt format documents).
- 2025/26 outcomes: Amarante FC champion (31 pts in 14 promotion-phase games) and promoted with Academica de Coimbra; Belenenses (3rd) lost the promotion playoff to Farense (16th of Liga 2) 0-1 on aggregate; relegated: 1.º Dezembro, Amora, Braga B, Sanjoanense (Wikipedia; abola.pt; dn.pt; JN).
- Status: Liga 3 is a semi-professional national league organized by the FPF (not by Liga Portugal, which runs the two professional tiers). B teams may compete and can be promoted to Liga 2 if they meet Liga Portugal criteria — Sporting B finished 2nd of the 2024/25 promotion phase and went up; Lusitania de Lourosa was 2024/25 champion (Wikipedia; maisfutebol.iol.pt).
- Real budget datapoint — Belenenses: budget 'na casa dos 650 mil euros' (~EUR 650,000) in their Liga 3 title season 2022/23, while promotion rivals had 'o triplo do orcamento' (~EUR 2M); after promotion, Liga Portugal licensing of the Estadio do Restelo alone required spending over EUR 500,000 (Observador / SAPO Desporto / Sportinforma, May 2023).
- Real budget datapoint — Academica de Coimbra: the 2022 SAD investment agreement (US investor, initial injection of almost EUR 5M) guaranteed a minimum annual football budget of EUR 1.2M for Liga 3 (Sportinforma, 2022).
- Real budget datapoint — Varzim SC: for 2025/26 the club budget was approved with a forecast surplus of EUR 499,000, while the SDUQ budget (senior A and B football teams) showed a deficit of EUR 494,000 — combined forecast balance +EUR 4,300; an earlier general assembly (season attribution uncertain, likely 2024/25) approved EUR 746,000 revenues and EUR 558,000 expenses plus EUR 223,000 in creditor-agreement payments (MAIS/Semanario).
- Real accounts datapoint — Caldas SC (fiscal year 2022/23, Liga 3): forecast result in the red by EUR 64,000, with costs having risen by about EUR 360,000 unmatched by revenue; club later opened talks (via KPMG) to attract investors/SAD (Gazeta das Caldas).
- Distress datapoint — Sanjoanense: the SAD investor (Urgel Martins) stopped funding in 2023, budget was cut and the coach walked away; the club ran 'strong budget limitations with one of the youngest squads' and was ultimately relegated in 2025/26 (O Jogo; Labor.pt).
- Typical budget range (estimate synthesized from the above real datapoints): roughly EUR 0.5M-1M for modest Liga 3 clubs, EUR 1.2M-2M for promotion contenders; no official league-wide budget survey exists.
- Player salaries — CCT minimums (Sindicato dos Jogadores, 2025/26): Liga 3 minimum salary = 1.5x RMMG (national minimum wage). With RMMG 2025 = EUR 870/month this is EUR 1,305/month gross (14 payments/year in Portugal); clubs that keep at least 50% of the first-team squad on registered sports employment contracts may instead apply the bare RMMG (EUR 870/month). RMMG rises to EUR 920 in 2026 (Decreto-Lei 139/2025), lifting the 1.5x floor to EUR 1,380/month (sjogadores.pt; portugal.gov.pt).
- Actual pay levels above the floor are not systematically published; press and union material indicate lower-league salaries are commonly minimum-to-low four figures per month plus match/objective bonuses (adeptosdebancada.com; sjogadores.pt) — treat league-average salary as estimate only.
- Registration rule affecting wage bills: if 11 registered players are professionals and the club is compliant with financial obligations on 31 December, 2 additional professional players may be registered (Sindicato dos Jogadores, 'Limite de registos e composicao dos planteis').
- Prize money / distributions: no public per-club Liga 3 prize-money table was found in any season; the FPF end-of-season 'Premios Liga 3' are trophies/individual awards (Best Player, Revelation, Team of the Year), not cash tables (Record; FPF news). Club-side league income at this level, per Belenenses' leadership (2023), comes from TV rights, betting-related money (Placard), sponsors, prizes, season tickets, member quotas and gate receipts — individual amounts undisclosed.
- Betting money context: FPF received EUR 38.8M from the online-betting tax (IEJO) in calendar 2025 (down EUR 4.5M vs 2024); Liga Portugal (LPFP) received EUR 16.24M in 2025; the Placard product generated EUR 16.5M for distribution in 2023. Placard is the Liga 3 title sponsor; how much trickles to each Liga 3 club is not published (eco.sapo.pt; Record; dn.pt).
- Travel support: the FPF received EUR 1.6M (public funds) to cover mainland clubs' travel to the Azores and Madeira in FPF competitions, and channels EUR 1M through an infrastructure support fund (Record; soccerway news).
- TV/streaming: Liga 3 matches are broadcast/streamed by the FPF's Canal 11 (free to viewers in Portugal via app and cable operators MEO/NOS/Nowo/Vodafone; paid subscription internationally). The FPF requires participating clubs to have stadium conditions for TV production, seats for spectators and natural grass; no evidence was found that clubs receive a significant TV rights fee — Belenenses listed 'direitos televisivos' among Liga 3 revenue but without amounts (fpf.pt editorial; Canal 11 FAQ; Observador).
- Promotion licensing to Liga Portugal 2: the promoted club must operate through a sociedade desportiva — SDUQ with minimum share capital EUR 50,000 or SAD with minimum EUR 200,000 for Liga 2 (Decreto-Lei 10/2013 regime; for I Liga: SDUQ EUR 250,000 / SAD EUR 1,000,000) — plus Liga Portugal stadium licensing including artificial floodlighting of 1,200 lux with generator backup, TV master camera position, flash-interview zone, media areas and sector segregation; Belenenses' Restelo compliance was quoted at over EUR 500,000 in 2023 (24noticias.sapo.pt; ligaportugal.pt licensing docs; Observador).
- Attendances (gate-revenue potential), 2023/24: Lusitania de Lourosa averaged 2,595 and Varzim 2,492 (both in Portugal's top-25 across all tiers); one-off peaks include Academica-Caldas with 6,356 spectators; most Liga 3 crowds are in the hundreds (A Bola, May 2024; CNN Portugal).
- Squad sizes and staff: promotion-phase squads run 25-30 players (Felgueiras went up in 2023/24 and registered 28 players, Transfermarkt squad value EUR 6.25M in Liga 2 in Jan 2025 — an outlier; typical Liga 3 squad market values are far lower). Technical staff at this level is typically a head coach (holder of an FPF/IPDJ coaching cedula), 2-3 assistants, GK coach and physio — league-wide staffing data is not published (Semanario de Felgueiras; Transfermarkt; FPF regulations).
- Rare windfall example: Amora FC (Liga 3) received about EUR 2.4M from Sporting CP for 75% of Geny Catamo's economic rights in December 2023 (other reports: EUR 2.25M for 65%, taking Sporting to 90%) — cited as the only Liga 3 club receiving big-club transfer money that window (Record; O Jogo; bolanarede.pt).

## Liga Portugal 2 (Portuguese second tier) — club budgets, TV money, league distributions, salaries/CCT, attendances, and licensing/infrastructure requirements

- Aggregate club economics (Liga Portugal/EY anuario data reported by Maisfutebol): in 2022/23 the II Liga's sports companies had total revenues of EUR 42M (average EUR 2.61M per club/SAD) against total costs of EUR 63M (average EUR 3.95M per club), i.e. the average Liga 2 club spends roughly 1.5x what it earns; total assets fell 7% to EUR 45M (average EUR 2.78M).
- In 2023/24 the 34 clubs of I+II Liga reached record combined revenues of EUR 1,073M, of which EUR 1,024M came from the 18 I Liga clubs — implying only about EUR 49M (~4.6%) for the entire II Liga (16 SADs), roughly EUR 3M average revenue per club (Liga Portugal/EY anuario via DN, RTP, Sportinforma).
- Individual club budget datapoints: Leixoes SAD's approved budget for 2017/18 in Liga 2 was EUR 1.955M of income vs EUR 1.950M of expenses, covering first team, B team and U19/U18 squads, including a EUR 102k protocol payment to the mother club (SAPO Desporto).
- Belenenses won promotion from Liga 3 to Liga 2 in 2023 with a budget of about EUR 650k (opponents reportedly had ~3x that); its president said Liga 2 entry required creating a SDUQ and spending over EUR 500k just to license Estadio do Restelo for Liga 2 (Observador/SAPO, May 2023).
- Older full-league budget survey (2010/11 Liga de Honra, Sportinforma): combined budgets ~EUR 22.5M for 16 clubs, average EUR 1.4M per club, with four clubs (Belenenses, Trofense, Leixoes, Oliveirense) above EUR 2M — orders of magnitude that remain representative of the mid-table today.
- Maritimo (Liga 2 since 2023/24, the biggest club in the division) is the best-documented case: Maritimo da Madeira Futebol SAD posted a net loss of EUR 4.496M in 2023/24 (first Liga 2 season, some outlets report 4.4-4.8M depending on consolidation) and a EUR 2.3-2.5M loss in 2024/25, with equity around EUR 12.5M; the club association side posted a EUR 398k profit in 2024/25 (Record/Sapo/DNoticias; full Relatorio e Contas PDFs at csmaritimo.org.pt).
- TV rights, historical benchmark: the centralized MEO/Altice deal for II Liga 2016/17-2018/19 was worth ~EUR 10.85M per year total, i.e. EUR 1.5M per club over 3 seasons (~EUR 500-570k per club per season), B teams excluded; promotion to I Liga tripled a club's TV money to ~EUR 3.5M (Observador, TSF, Finance Football, Jan 2016).
- TV rights today: since that deal lapsed clubs sell rights individually and Liga 2 TV income is far lower and unevenly distributed; mandatory centralization was approved by government decree from 2028/29 with a 90%/10% split between I and II Liga — at a EUR 225M/season market value that is EUR 22.5M/season for II Liga (~EUR 1.25M per club), at the current ~EUR 180M market value EUR 18M (~EUR 1M per club) (A Bola Apr 2026, Forbes PT, RTP/Sportinforma).
- League/UEFA distributions: Liga Portugal distributes UEFA solidarity money equally to Liga 2 — in 2024/25 EUR 6.119M total, i.e. EUR 382,437 per club for the 16 SADs (Benfica B and FC Porto B excluded); the equal-distribution model was reconfirmed unanimously in Sep 2024 (Record, A Bola, RTP).
- Liga Portugal's own budget distributed >EUR 9.8M in dividends to the 34 SADs of both tiers in 2023/24 (Liga closed the season with EUR 774k profit), ~EUR 9M planned for 2023/24, and ~EUR 10M in 2025/26 out of a record EUR 31.8M Liga budget (Record/Sapo/Jornal Economico) — the II Liga per-SAD share is not published separately.
- CCT minimum wage: the collective agreement between Liga Portugal and the Sindicato dos Jogadores sets minimum player salaries as multiples of the Portuguese national minimum wage (RMMG): Liga Portugal Betclic 3x RMMG, Liga Portugal 2 1.75x RMMG, Liga 3 1.5x RMMG, Campeonato de Portugal 1x RMMG (2025/26 values, sjogadores.pt); with RMMG at EUR 870/month in 2025 the Liga 2 minimum is ~EUR 1,522.50/month (~EUR 21,315/year on 14 payments), rising to ~EUR 1,610/month with the EUR 920 RMMG of 2026; when RMMG was EUR 557 (2017/18) the Liga 2 minimum was EUR 974.75/month (Record).
- Average salaries: the average II Liga player wage was reported at ~EUR 1,000/month in 2017 (Diario de Noticias, old estimate); the 2023/24 anuario shows total remunerations across both professional leagues of EUR 424M (players EUR 294M), with II Liga players paying EUR 9M of IRS income tax vs EUR 115M by I Liga players and ~EUR 2M vs EUR 20M in social security — confirming a wage gap of roughly 10x between the tiers.
- Wage bills/wage share: no official per-club wage-bill table is published for Liga 2, but the 2022/23 averages (EUR 3.95M costs vs EUR 2.61M revenue per club) and Maritimo's accounts indicate wages are the dominant cost line and typically exceed 50-60% of revenue; the league itself called the situation 'not healthy' with liabilities at record highs (Maisfutebol, 'II Liga esta pior financeiramente').
- Attendances: Liga 2 averaged 1,818 spectators per match in 2023/24 (total 555,575, up 64.7% year-on-year — a record driven by Maritimo avg 7,320 and Uniao de Leiria avg 5,771, both in the national top 25); combined I+II Liga attendance hit 4,263,585 in 2023/24 and 3.76M+ in the I Liga alone in 2024/25 (A Bola, Publico, Record, Liga Portugal).
- Licensing/legal form: under Decreto-Lei 10/2013 every club in professional competitions (I and II Liga) must since 2013/14 operate its football through a sports company — SAD (share company) or SDUQ (single-member limited company wholly owned by the club); minimum share capital for II Liga participation is EUR 200,000 for a SAD and EUR 50,000 for a SDUQ (vs EUR 1,000,000 / EUR 250,000 for I Liga).
- Stadium/infrastructure: Liga Portugal licensing requires floodlighting (I Liga-level references cite at least 1,200 lux with backup power), FIFA-quality natural or certified artificial turf, delimited technical areas, media facilities, a first-aid post and an ambulance present on matchdays; upgrading a stadium to Liga 2 licensing standard cost Belenenses over EUR 500k in 2023 (Liga Portugal Regulamento das Competicoes 2025/26; Observador).
- Coaching staff: per the amended Art. 82 of the Regulamento das Competicoes (in force 2025/26), Liga 2 head coaches must hold UEFA Pro (Grau IV), but a team promoted into Liga Portugal 2 may field a head coach with only UEFA Advanced (Grau III) in the season following promotion; coaches of teams promoted to the I Liga must at least prove enrolment in the UEFA Pro course (ligaportugal.pt news 24877).
- Macro context: Portuguese professional football (both leagues) contributed EUR 956M to the economy and paid EUR 288M in taxes in 2024/25 (Liga Portugal/EY anuario via O Jogo, RTP, Correio da Manha); B teams (Benfica B, Porto B) participate in Liga 2 but are excluded from league distributions because they belong to I Liga SADs.
- Practical budget benchmark for a promoted club: guaranteed central income for a Liga 2 club in 2024/25 was roughly EUR 380-450k (EUR 382,437 UEFA solidarity + small Liga dividends), so a typical low-end competitive Liga 2 budget of EUR 1.5-2.5M must be mostly self-funded from sponsors, gate receipts, player trading and owners; four to five clubs (Maritimo, Tondela-type relegated clubs, foreign-owned projects like Academico de Viseu/HOBRA-Dietmar Hopp 51%) operate at EUR 4M+.

## Cost structure and staffing of a professional football club — Portuguese 2nd/3rd tier benchmark (Liga Portugal 2 / Liga 3)

- Liga Portugal 2 club budgets averaged about EUR 1.4M per club (total ~EUR 22.5M across 16 clubs, only four clubs above EUR 2M) per a Sportinforma analysis; by 2023/24 the EY/Liga Portugal Anuario implies average II Liga club revenue of roughly EUR 3M (I+II Liga combined revenues EUR 1,073M of which I Liga EUR 1,024M, leaving ~EUR 49M for 16 II Liga clubs).
- A concrete Liga 3 benchmark: Varzim SC's football company (Varzim SDUQ, running the pro squad and B team) lost EUR 1.05M in 2023/24, EUR 820k in 2024/25, and budgeted a EUR 494k deficit for 2025/26, partly offset by ~EUR 200k municipal support and EUR 70k FPF support — indicating a Liga 3 football operation of roughly EUR 1.2-1.8M in annual costs.
- Collectively agreed minimum monthly wages for players 2025/26 (SJPF/Liga CCT), based on RMMG of EUR 870 in 2025 rising to EUR 920 in 2026: Liga 1 = 3x RMMG (EUR 2,610), Liga 2 = 1.75x RMMG (EUR 1,522.50), Liga 3 = 1.5x RMMG (EUR 1,305), Campeonato de Portugal = 1x RMMG; Liga 3 clubs keeping at least 50% of the squad on registered employment contracts may pay 1x RMMG (EUR 870).
- Head coaches in Liga 2 have a collectively agreed minimum of 4x the national minimum wage (EUR 3,480/month at 2025 RMMG of EUR 870) under the LPFP-ANTF collective agreement, with assistant coaches guaranteed half the head-coach minimum (EUR 1,740/month); in practice testimony indicates some Liga 2 coaches earn around EUR 1,000/month at Liga 3/CP level structures, with pay built as base salary plus promotion/classification bonuses.
- Social security for professional players uses a special regime (Codigo dos Regimes Contributivos): the contribution base is one-fifth (1/5) of effective remuneration (floor of 1x IAS), taxed at 33.3% total (22.3% employer / 11% player) — so the effective employer charge is only ~4.5% of a player's gross pay; all NON-playing staff (coaches without player status arrangements, medical, admin, commercial) fall under the standard TSU of 34.75% (23.75% employer + 11% employee).
- UEFA's Financial Sustainability rules set a 70% squad-cost-to-revenue target (the widely used 60-70% wage-bill rule of thumb); 2021 league wage/revenue ratios were Bundesliga 65%, Premier League 71%, La Liga 74%, Serie A 84%, Ligue 1 98%, and across the Portuguese professional leagues 2023/24 player salaries were EUR 294M with total salaries (league + clubs) of EUR 424M against EUR 1,073M revenues (~40% players-only, ~55%+ all wages at II Liga level where revenues are thin).
- Refereeing cost professional Portuguese clubs (I+II Liga, 34 clubs) EUR 4.95M in total in 2024/25 (+15.7% vs EUR ~4.28M in 2023/24), i.e., an average of ~EUR 145k per club per season; per-match referee fees reported: I Liga ~EUR 1,090-1,342, II Liga ~EUR 800, Taca de Portugal EUR 560, fourth official 25% of the main fee, mileage EUR 0.35-0.38/km, plus EUR 400/month training subsidy for referees.
- Matchday security costs: clubs pay PSP special-unit policing at EUR 40-54 per officer per 4-hour shift; licensed stadium stewards (ARD - Assistente de Recinto Desportivo) earn EUR 9.44-9.98/hour under the 2024 private-security collective agreement (billed agency rates are higher); in non-professional football the state (MAI) co-funds policing at 90/80/50% depending on level, with the remainder paid by the club.
- Liga 3 match regulations (FPF, 2025/26) require an ambulance with defibrillator (AED) and a basic-life-support technician at every match; volunteer fire-brigade ambulance standby rates run about EUR 30 base for the first hour plus EUR 0.60/km beyond 20 km and ~EUR 8/hour per crew member waiting time (older published price table - budget EUR 100-300 per match at current prices).
- Portuguese clubs paid EUR 82.4M in agent/intermediary commissions between April 2022 and March 2023 (FPF report); the only II Liga club in the national top 10 was UD Oliveirense at EUR 750k, indicating typical Liga 2 clubs spend tens to low hundreds of thousands of EUR and Liga 3 clubs far less.
- Travel costs: team coach hire runs EUR 300-450 for a local minicoach and EUR 600-900 for a full coach on a ~3-hour away trip; Liga 3 rules make the visiting club bear travel costs above the limits fixed in FPF Comunicado Oficial n.1, and island fixtures (Madeira/Azores) require commercial flights for a ~25-person delegation since resident air-fare subsidies do not apply to clubs.
- Stadium costs at this level are usually municipal: examples range from free concession (Coimbra municipal stadium to AFC, 4-year contract) to symbolic rents (~EUR 500/month in one reported contract); building a natural grass pitch costs ~EUR 120k and artificial EUR 100-150k, with professional annual natural-pitch maintenance (mowing, irrigation, scarification, fertilizer) having no published Portuguese club figure (estimate EUR 30-80k/year).
- Mandatory insurance: every club must transfer work-accident liability for players and staff to an insurer (players on full salary while injured are the club's cost), with premiums set as a rate on payroll adjusted to activity risk - football rates are materially higher than office rates (no published club premium; estimate 2-6% of player payroll).
- Club licensing (Liga Portugal manual, aligned with UEFA/FPF) requires as minimum staff: a doctor recognized by the Ordem dos Medicos and registered with FPF/LPFP, a registered physiotherapist, a general director, a qualified press/media officer, a fans' liaison officer (OLA), a security director, and a UEFA Pro licensed head coach for I and II Liga (Liga 3 head coaches also prioritized for UEFA Pro courses); II Liga clubs must be sports companies (SAD/SDUQ), as must Liga 3 clubs; administrative licensing fees are minor (e.g., certification EUR 56.70+VAT, inspection EUR 153+VAT per Liga CO 01 2025/26).
- Employment benchmark from the EY/Liga Portugal Anuario 2023/24: professional football employed over 4,000 people; the 18 I Liga companies employed 3,239 (989 players, 322 coaches, 1,928 support/management/admin staff = 73% of the total), implying II Liga clubs average roughly 40-60 employees each including ~25-30 players, a technical staff of 6-9, and a civilian structure of 10-25.
- Benchmark monthly gross salaries for non-football staff in Portugal (2025-26): national average wage EUR 1,694 (INE 2025); physiotherapist EUR 1,150-1,500 general market (EUR 35-50k/year only at Benfica/Porto/Sporting level); accountant EUR 968-1,374 early career and EUR 1,138-1,715 with 5+ years; administrative assistant ~EUR 1,000-1,368; marketing professional ~EUR 1,507 average with digital marketing managers at ~EUR 2,180; stewards EUR ~9.4-10.0/hour - a Liga 2/3 club can staff admin/media/commercial roles at EUR 1,100-2,200 gross/month per head (plus 23.75% TSU and 14 monthly payments/year).
- Revenue-side context for the budget: Liga Portugal distributed ~EUR 10M to I+II Liga clubs from its record EUR 31.8M budget in 2025/26; a November 2025 FPF-Liga protocol change adds over EUR 2.3M for clubs; and from 2028/29 centralized TV rights (~EUR 225M/season) will allocate 10% to II Liga (~EUR 22.5M, roughly EUR 1.25M per club average), while Liga 3 clubs rely on FPF support (Varzim received EUR 70k), Canal 11 exposure, gate receipts, and municipal contract-programs.
- Minimum wage trajectory matters for multi-year budgeting because football minimums are indexed to it: RMMG rises EUR 870 (2025), EUR 920 (2026), EUR 970 (2027), EUR 1,020 (2028), reaching EUR 1,100 by 2029 - automatically inflating the wage floors for players (1.5-1.75x) and head coaches (4x) by ~5.5% per year.

## Portuguese promotion case studies: club budgets in Liga 3, Liga Portugal 2 and Primeira Liga (2021-2026) — promotion-season budgets, post-promotion jumps, budget-vs-position, overspending/insolvency cases, Varzim SC's relegation-era finances, and realistic 'candidato a subida' budget levels

- PROMOTIONS LIGA 3 -> LIGA 2, corrected list: 2021/22 Torreense (champion); 2022/23 Uniao de Leiria (champion) + Belenenses + Lank Vilaverdense (playoff win over B SAD); 2023/24 Alverca (champion, 30 pts in promotion phase) + Felgueiras (2nd, 24 pts); 2024/25 Lusitania de Lourosa (champion) + Sporting B (2nd), Belenenses lost the playoff to Pacos de Ferreira. NOTE: AVS was NEVER promoted from Liga 3 (its SAD bought the ex-Vilafranquense Liga 2 place and moved to Vila das Aves in May 2023), and Vianense was never promoted Liga 3->Liga 2 (it was relegated from Liga 3 in 2023/24 and only returned to Liga 3 via the Campeonato de Portugal, promotion confirmed 31 May 2026).
- LIGA 3 PROMOTION BUDGET BENCHMARK: Belenenses won promotion to Liga 2 in 2022/23 with a budget of about EUR 650,000 while, per president Patrick Morais de Carvalho, direct rivals had roughly triple that (~EUR 2M); after promotion the club still had 'very probably the lowest budget in II Liga' because LPFP licensing of Estadio do Restelo alone cost over EUR 500,000 (Observador/SAPO, May 2023). This implies a realistic Liga 3 'candidato a subida' budget of roughly EUR 1.5-2M, with EUR 0.65M the proven minimum.
- LIGA 2 BUDGET REALITY (EY/Liga Portugal report for 2022/23, via Maisfutebol): II Liga sports societies' aggregate revenues were EUR 42M (down 10% YoY) against EUR 63M of expenses; average revenue per club EUR 2.6M vs average cost per club ~EUR 4M; aggregate liabilities hit a record EUR 97M (+24% vs 2021/22). Structural overspending is the norm in Liga 2, and Luis Obral (Substack, 6 Jun 2025) counts 10 of the 16 non-B-team Liga 2 SADs of 2024/25 as having or arranging foreign money; ECO (7 Aug 2025) reports 58% of Portuguese professional teams have foreign shareholders.
- LIGA 2 'CANDIDATO A SUBIDA' BUDGET: no outlet publishes an official threshold, but the observed pattern is that promotion contenders spend 2-3x the league's EUR 2.6M average revenue: Torreense's SAD reported operational spending of EUR 8.2M in 2024/25 and around EUR 9M in the season it fought for I Liga promotion (CM/Record, 2025-26 reporting); Liga 2 2023/24 was won by the two parachute clubs Santa Clara (73 pts) and Nacional (71 pts) which finished 1-2, and 3rd-placed AVS also went up via playoff; Liga 2 2024/25 was won by parachute club Tondela with Vinicius-Jr-funded Alverca 2nd. In every season 2022/23-2024/25 the top of Liga 2 was occupied by relegated clubs with parachute budgets or investor-funded SADs.
- CASA PIA BUDGET JUMP (Liga 2 -> Primeira 2022): budget slightly under EUR 2M in the 2021/22 promotion season (Diario de Noticias), rising to EUR 5M in the Primeira Liga in 2022/23 (foradejogo.pt/Jornal Economico) and staying at EUR 5M in 2023/24 (bomdia.eu) - a ~2.5x jump on promotion; despite being among the lowest budgets it finished comfortably mid-table in 2022/23. Owner: US investor Robert Platek (entered end-2020).
- ESTRELA DA AMADORA (promoted to Primeira 2023 via playoff): I Liga budget EUR 7M in 2023/24 (bomdia.eu list) - but by 2026 the SAD's court-recognised debts reached EUR 25.4M (Record), it filed a PER asking creditors to forgive 90% (over EUR 20M, ECO 22 May 2026) to avoid exclusion from professional football, and the PER was rejected ('chumbado', Record). Main creditors: Flamengo EUR 5.7M, tax authority EUR 3.8M, MYFC EUR 3.6M, SAD president Paulo Lopo ~EUR 2M. A textbook post-promotion overspend.
- NACIONAL (promoted 2023/24 as Liga 2 runner-up): president Rui Alves said the planned budget for the 2024/25 Primeira Liga season was EUR 7M and he did not know if it would be enough (RTP, summer 2024); by 2025/26 Nacional had the 10th-highest I Liga payroll at EUR 6.435M budgeted (Sportingpedia via dnoticias.pt, Oct 2025).
- TONDELA AND ALVERCA (promoted to Primeira 2025): Tondela won Liga 2 2024/25 and has historically operated the smallest top-flight budget (EUR 3M in 2021/22, the I Liga's lowest - Jornal do Centro, Aug 2021; for scale, Maritimo invested EUR 12M that year, 4x Tondela); after its club sale about EUR 4M was earmarked for the academy. Alverca won Liga 3 in 2023/24 (champion) and reached Primeira in 2024/25 as Liga 2 runner-up after a consortium around Real Madrid's Vinicius Junior took shareholder control in Dec 2024, buying Ricardo Vicintin's stake for up to EUR 10M (Feb 2025); for 2025/26 it signed 26 players and carries a squad market value of EUR 15.82M (Flashscore/Transfermarkt estimate).
- AVS (promoted to Primeira 2024): founded 5 May 2023 when the ex-Vilafranquense SAD relocated to Vila das Aves; finished 3rd in Liga 2 2023/24 and beat Portimonense in the playoff; A Bola (Mar 2024) described the project as 'a fundo, mas sem derrapagens' (all-in but without overruns); finished 16th in 2024/25 and survived via relegation playoff. No public absolute budget figure was found for AVS in any season - a genuine reporting gap.
- DOES THE BIGGEST BUDGET WIN? In the Primeira Liga 2023/24 the 18 clubs' combined budgets were ~EUR 400M (Record/bomdia.eu) with Benfica's the largest (EUR 115M cited for 2024/25, em-defesa-do-benfica compilation) - yet Sporting, not Benfica, won both 2023/24 and 2024/25 titles, so the top budget did not win. At the bottom, joint-lowest-budget Moreirense (EUR 4.5M in 2023/24) finished in the top half, while Boavista (also EUR 4.5M) fought relegation. In Liga 2 and Liga 3, budget rank predicts the top much better: parachute/investor clubs took every automatic promotion slot 2022/23-2024/25 except Felgueiras 2023/24 and Belenenses 2022/23, the two famous small-budget exceptions. Moreirense's president also provided the counterexample quote after the 2021/22 relegation: 'Tivemos o maior orcamento de sempre, mas andou tudo ao contrario' (biggest budget ever, still went down - Sportinforma/DNoticias, Jun 2022).
- LANK VILAVERDENSE - PROMOTION THEN COLLAPSE: promoted from Liga 3 in 2022/23 via playoff (first professional season ever); finished 17th of Liga 2 in 2023/24 (8W-4D-22L) and was directly relegated; in July 2024 the Lank Football Group abandoned the project amid financial difficulties and incoming Brazilian-Japanese investors assumed about EUR 750,000 of debts in wages, bonuses, suppliers and state obligations (O Vilaverdense).
- UNIAO DE LEIRIA - BOTH ERAS: the 2011/12 collapse chasing the I Liga ended with insolvency listing 107 creditors and EUR 13.5M of claims (state alone >EUR 3M), 16 players rescinding contracts, and relegation to district level (RTP/Jornal de Negocios). The reborn club won Liga 3 in 2022/23 after investors spent 'some millions'; in Liga 2 its 2023/24 planning was hit when ~EUR 450k of budgeted UEFA-solidarity revenue was not distributed to Liga 2 clubs, it paid April-May 2024 salary arrears on the last day to secure registration (Jornal de Leiria), and by July 2026 faced seizures ('penhoras') and an insolvency petition (Regiao de Leiria).
- ACADEMICA - COST OF DROPPING OUT OF LIGA 2: relegated to Liga 3 in 2021/22 after 88 years in the top two tiers; filed insolvency-with-recovery in Sept 2022 with debts over EUR 5.6M (EUR 1.9M to the state, EUR 3.1M to suppliers) and EUR 8M negative equity; the drop to Liga 3 cost about EUR 1.5M per year in TV, betting and Santa Casa revenue - a good proxy for the central-revenue value of a Liga 2 place; creditors approved the plan in Dec 2023: ~EUR 7M repaid over 12.5 years (Observador/Publico/A Bola/Record).
- OTHER COLLAPSES: Cova da Piedade was administratively relegated from Liga 2 in 2021 for a 3-day-late registration and its Chinese-majority SAD was declared insolvent by the Barreiro court in 2022 after failing FPF certification (CNN Portugal/Jornal de Negocios). B-SAD (ex-Belenenses SAD) slid I Liga->Liga 2->Liga 3, lost the 2023 playoff to Lank Vilaverdense, was excluded from national leagues after a failed merger with Cova da Piedade and dissolved in Sept 2024. Boavista, with accumulated debts over EUR 150M, was refused the 2025/26 professional licence, fell administratively toward district level, had liquidation approved in Sept 2025 and ceased activity in July 2026 despite having paid the state almost EUR 5M since June 2024 (Observador/DN/futebol365). Vitoria FC (Setubal) also ran a court recovery plan while in Liga 3.
- VARZIM SC - CORRECTED TIMELINE AND FIGURES: Varzim was relegated from Liga 2 in 2021/22 (17th, alongside Academica), not 2022/23; in 2022/23-2025/26 it has played Liga 3. Its 2021/22 accounts (1 Jul 2021-30 Jun 2022) showed the club roughly at break-even (-EUR 12k) but the football SDUQ lost EUR 1.1M; the 2022/23 (first Liga 3 season) budget targeted club +EUR 7k and an SDUQ loss cut to EUR 346k (Voz da Povoa/Mais Semanario). In summer 2022 president Edgar Pinho personally covered ~EUR 500k to pay salaries and secure registration. By Jan 2024 the board resigned leaving ~EUR 6.5M of liabilities, players had 3 months of unpaid wages and protested on the pitch (A Bola/Record, Feb 2024), and an administrative commission ran the club; new president Ricardo Nunes (elected 2024, 228 of 247 votes) said Varzim had 'um orcamento irrealista para a realidade do clube' and had lived above its means for 20 years, launching a PER to avoid insolvency (Renascenca/Record).
- FELGUEIRAS - SMALL-BUDGET SURVIVAL MODEL: promoted from Liga 3 in 2023/24 (2nd behind Alverca) after 19 years away from tier 2; president Reinaldo Teixeira openly ran one of the smallest budgets in Liga 2 2024/25, arguing budget size 'does not determine results' (Felgueiras Magazine); it mathematically secured Liga 2 survival at matchday 33 of 34 (RTP/Liga Portugal) and stayed up again toward 2025/26.
- LUSITANIA DE LOUROSA AND SPORTING B (promoted 2024/25): Lourosa won the Liga 3 title (30 pts in the promotion phase vs Sporting B's 26) but entered Liga 2 2025/26 with a consolidation-only goal against rivals with 'differentiated budgets' and its stadium unfinished (president targeted October 2025 completion, O Jogo) - no absolute budget figure published; Sporting B is a B-team funded inside Sporting CP's budget with no standalone published orcamento.
- CENTRAL REVENUE CONTEXT FOR PROMOTED CLUBS: Liga Portugal (the league body) distributed a record EUR 9.8M to I+II Liga sports societies in 2024/25 and ~EUR 10M in 2025/26 out of league budgets of EUR 31.5M/31.8M, with >EUR 11M planned for 2026/27; Portuguese professional football's aggregate club revenue passed EUR 1.073 billion for the first time in 2023/24 (EY/Liga Portugal). A promoted Liga 3 club entering Liga 2 gains roughly the EUR 1.5M/year of TV/betting/Santa Casa-linked income that Academica lost going the other way.

**Caveats:** 1) Access constraints: the sandbox egress proxy blocked direct WebFetch to nearly all Portuguese outlets (record.pt, abola.pt, ojogo.pt, zerozero.pt, publico.pt, observador.pt, rr.pt, sapo/eco, wikipedia, club sites), so every figure was extracted from WebSearch result summaries of those pages rather than the full articles; wording-level precision (e.g. whether a budget is SAD-only or club+SAD) could not always be verified. 2) Portuguese club 'orcamentos' quoted in media (bomdia.eu, foradejogo, Record fotogaleria) are self-declared season budgets, not audited accounts, and outlets differ on definitions; treat them as order-of-magnitude estimates. Casa Pia EUR 5M was cross-checked across two independent lists; Benfica EUR 115M (2024/25) comes from a fan-blog compilation and is weaker. 3) No published absolute budget was found for: AVS (any season), Felgueiras, Lusitania de Lourosa, Lank Vilaverdense's promotion season, Alverca's Liga 3/Liga 2 seasons, or Estrela da Amadora's Liga 2 promotion season - only qualitative claims ('one of the lowest', investor sums, debts). Liga 3 club budgets are almost never published; the Belenenses EUR 650k figure and its 'rivals had triple' quote is the best documented benchmark. 4) Corrections to the request's premises: Varzim's relegation from Liga 2 was 2021/22 (17th place), not 2022/23 - budget figures given are for that season and the 2022/23 Liga 3 season; AVS never played Liga 3 (bought a Liga 2 place); Vianense was relegated from Liga 3 in 2023/24 and returned via Campeonato de Portugal in May 2026 (conflicting 2025 headlines suggest a failed first attempt in 2024/25 - promotion date verified only from Diario do Minho 31 May 2026); 'Tondela 2025' and 'Alverca 2025' are correct Primeira promotions. 5) The 2023/24 'EUR 42M revenue vs EUR 63M expenses' EY figures refer to 2022/23 accounts published during 2023/24; season attribution in the Maisfutebol summary is slightly ambiguous. 6) The 'biggest budget wins?' analysis for Liga 2/Liga 3 is inferential (based on who was promoted and known parachute/investor status), not from a formal budget-rank table, since full Liga 2/Liga 3 budget tables are not published. 7) Some sources date from 2026 (Estrela PER rejection, Boavista closure, U. Leiria penhoras, Torreense Europa League) and reflect situations still evolving as of Aug 2026.

## Taça de Portugal per-round prize money, Taça da Liga eligibility, and FPF/Liga 3 participation subsidies (for Varzim SC promotion budget)

- Taça de Portugal 2025/26 (FPF Comunicado Oficial n.74, 06.08.2025, reported by Maisfutebol/CNN Portugal/JN/Flashscore): total prize pot EUR 2,322,000, paid as CUMULATIVE per-round participation prizes - 1.a eliminatoria EUR 4,000 per club, 2.a EUR 5,000, 3.a EUR 6,000, 4.a EUR 8,000, 5.a (oitavos) EUR 12,000, 6.a (quartos) EUR 16,000, 7.a (meias-finais) EUR 22,500, final: winner EUR 325,000 and losing finalist EUR 175,000.
- There are NO separate win bonuses in the Taca de Portugal: each round reached pays its participation amount and amounts accumulate along a run, so e.g. a Liga 3 club eliminated in the 3.a eliminatoria collects 4,000+5,000+6,000 = EUR 15,000 across the season; TV transmission fees are paid on top of these amounts (Maisfutebol/zerozero, Aug 2025).
- Taça de Portugal 2024/25 used the SAME per-club round values (4k/5k/6k/8k/12k/16k/22.5k; winner 325k, runner-up 175k) - confirmed by Record, CNN Portugal and bolanarede.pt (Aug 2024); total FPF investment in the 2024/25 edition was EUR 4.698M (+24% vs 2023/24, highest ever), the larger total including TV-transmission payments on top of the EUR ~2.3M participation-prize pot.
- TV transmission payments 2024/25 (per match shown on free-to-air or premium channel, on top of participation prizes): 3.a eliminatoria EUR 60,000; 4.a EUR 90,000; 5.a EUR 120,000; 6.a EUR 150,000; 7.a EUR 200,000; final EUR 350,000 (bolanarede.pt/Record, Aug 2024) - relevant for Varzim only if its tie is picked for broadcast; how the fee is split between the two clubs was not specified in accessible sources.
- The 'EUR 6M for the cup winner' figure cited around Torreense is NOT the FPF prize: Torreense (Liga 2) won the 2025/26 final vs Sporting and Correio da Manha's 'Vitoria na Taca de Portugal rende 6 milhoes ao Torreense' headline aggregates the FPF winner's cheque (EUR 325k) with UEFA Europa League league-phase entry money (EUR 4.31M participation alone, per Record) plus TV and other windfalls.
- Entry point relevant to Varzim: Liga 3 clubs enter the Taca de Portugal at the 1.a eliminatoria (2026/27 draw of 18.08.2026: 94 teams enter - 42 district clubs, 52 of 55 Campeonato de Portugal teams, 19 of 20 Liga 3 teams, B-teams excluded, 19 byes); Liga 2 clubs enter at the 2.a; Primeira Liga clubs entered at the 3.a through 2025/26 and move to the 4.a from 2026/27 (zerozero/DN, Aug 2026).
- Taça de Portugal 2026/27 prize table: NOT yet published/found as of 18.08.2026 (the 1.a eliminatoria is played 29-30 Aug 2026); format changes are confirmed (single-leg semi-finals, Liga 1 entry moved to 4.a eliminatoria) but no new money values - for budgeting, carrying over the 2025/26 values (EUR 4k at 1.a etc.) is a reasonable assumption but is an ASSUMPTION, not a published figure.
- Taça da Liga (Allianz Cup): Liga 3 clubs are NOT eligible - the competition is contested exclusively by clubs of the two professional divisions (Liga Portugal 1 and 2), and since 2024/25 it is restricted to just 8 teams (top 6 of Liga 1 + top 2 of Liga 2 of the prior season), with B-teams also barred (Wikipedia/ogol/zerozero); a promoted Varzim would only touch this competition after reaching Liga 2 AND finishing top-2. Its per-club prize money is not publicly disclosed in any source found.
- Liga 3 FPF participation subsidy: NO league-wide public table exists - none of the FPF Liga 3 regulations, formato documents or press coverage found discloses a standard per-club participation payment; the FPF holds Liga 3 TV rights centrally (Canal 11/YouTube) with no published per-club TV compensation.
- Varzim-specific: MAIS/Semanario (Oct 2025) reported Varzim expected approximately EUR 70,000 in FPF support for the season (2025/26) - this is the only source found for the figure; its exact composition (participation support vs. Taca prizes vs. other funds) and whether every Liga 3 club receives a similar amount could NOT be verified from public sources.
- Liga 3 'Ranking Puro Futebol' (FPF/APCVD fair-play ranking) pays end-of-season prizes in three tiers (Ouro/Prata/Bronze, minimum 700 points to qualify; 2024/25 Ouro: Caldas with 1,165 points), but the monetary values are set by the FPF Direcao and are NOT publicly disclosed.
- No monetary prize for the Liga 3 champion or for promotion could be found in any public source - the 2024/25 Liga 3 'premios' ceremony (June 2025) covered only individual awards (best player, best goalkeeper, top scorer, revelation - won by Varzim's Claudio Araujo - fair play, team of the year); budget should assume EUR 0 unless FPF discloses otherwise.
- Placard/betting money: online sports-betting tax revenue (IEJO) is delivered to the FPF, which collected EUR 33.85M in 2024 while Liga Portugal received EUR 10.85M; there is NO earmarked, published distribution to Liga 3 clubs, and distribution criteria were still under FPF-Liga dispute/discussion as of Nov 2025 (A Bola/Publico/Record) - assume EUR 0 direct Placard revenue for a Liga 3 club.
- FPF collective supports that do NOT reach Liga 3 clubs as cash: the Nov 2025 FPF-Liga Portugal protocol change (+EUR 2.3M) benefits professional-league clubs only; UEFA solidarity/FPF distributions (EUR 6.662M to 30 Liga 1+2 clubs; EUR 7.5-8.5M to non-European Liga 1 clubs) exclude Liga 3; the FPF AG of Oct 2025 approved EUR 3.2M for non-professional football but as insurance and travel support, not per-club subsidies.
- Campeonato de Portugal comparison: CP clubs enter the Taca de Portugal at the same 1.a eliminatoria for the same EUR 4,000; the only club-support figure found is the 2020 COVID-era FPF fund allowing loans of up to EUR 28,000 per CP club (interest-free, repaid over 4 years, last 25% tranche forgivable) - a loan, not a recurring subsidy; no regular CP participation subsidy is published.
- Varzim budget context found en route: for 2026/27 Varzim projects ~EUR 1.75M revenues vs ~EUR 1.73M costs and enters the season debt-free after concluding PER processes for club and SDUQ (MAIS/Semanario, 2026) - so the realistic 'competition revenue' line for a Liga 3 season is roughly EUR 9,000-15,000 of Taca prizes (2 or 3 rounds) plus the unverified ~EUR 70,000 FPF support, i.e. about 5% of total revenue.

## Varzim SC budget composition: 2026/27 budget (~EUR 1.75M), municipal contrato-programa, sponsors, ticket/quota income, wage bill, squad market value

- Varzim's 2026/27 budget, presented to the AG of 19 June 2026 (Biblioteca Diana Bar, Povoa de Varzim), forecasts approximately EUR 1.75M in revenues and EUR 1.73M in expenses, covering both the club and the SDUQ whose budgets/activity plans were on the same agenda; NO public line-by-line breakdown (quotas, bilheteira, patrocinios, subsidios, transfers) was published by any outlet (MAIS/Semanario 'Sem dividas e com ambicao'; Caxinas TV 07-06-2026).
- The 2026/27 budget is about EUR 300k LOWER than the 2025/26 budget, implying a 2025/26 budget of roughly EUR 2.0-2.05M (derived); the board explicitly cited 'strong dependence on subsidies and limited revenues' and inability to compete financially with professional clubs (MAIS/Semanario, June 2026).
- With the conclusion of both PER processes (club and SDUQ), Varzim enters 2026/27 free of liabilities ('livre de passivo'), funded largely by the sale of the old training ground; socios approved the sale in Aug 2025 and the Camara Municipal approved its purchase for EUR 2.4M on 17 Feb 2026 (Caxinas TV; Radio Onda Viva 39515; MAIS/Semanario), pending Tribunal de Contas approval.

## Line-item P&L evidence for small/mid Liga Portugal 2 clubs and current per-club Liga 2 TV income (2024/25-2026/27, pre-centralization)

- Audited 'Relatorio e Contas' PDFs for small/mid Liga 2 clubs are publicly downloadable but could NOT be opened from this sandbox (egress proxy blocks all non-search domains): Maritimo SAD 2022/23 and 2023/24 (csmaritimo.org.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Maritimo-Futebol-SAD_Relatorio-e-Contas-30jun24.pdf and .../2024/01/MaritimoSAD_RC_30.06.2023.pdf), Torreense SAD 2021/22-2024/25 (torreense.com/files/SAD_RelatorioContas2024-2025V2.pdf etc.), Leixoes SAD 2023/24 and 2024/25 (leixoessad.pt/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Relatorio-e-Contas-Consolidado-2023-2024.pdf and .../2025/11/Relatorio-de-Gestao-e-Contas-2024-2025.pdf), Uniao de Leiria SAD 2023/24 (uniaodeleiria.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Relatorio-Contas-UDL-SAD_2023-2024.pdf), Santa Clara (cdsantaclara.com/prestacao-de-contas/); fetch these from an unrestricted network to get gastos com pessoal, FSE, bilheteira, patrocinios and the direitos-televisivos revenue line.
- Maritimo da Madeira Futebol SAD (Liga 2): audited net loss of EUR 4,496,269.21 in 2023/24 (first Liga 2 season, approved in AG 11 Oct 2024 with ~95% of capital present), versus a loss of just over EUR 300k in 2022/23 (last Liga 1 season); the board attributed the swing 'essencialmente' to the sharp fall in TV-rights revenue, a lower Madeira Regional Government contract (contrato-programa) and lower player-transfer gains, and said it cut personnel costs and FSE (sports-facility/stadium rents and away-match travel and lodging) in response - this is the clearest documented evidence that relegation from Liga 1 to Liga 2 destroys the TV line.
- Maritimo SAD 2024/25 (Liga 2): net loss ~EUR 2.3M (initially reported as EUR 2.5M by dnoticias on the report's release; the AG coverage and RTP Madeira/Madeirafutebol give 2.3M), approved by ~92.3% of shareholders; equity (capitais proprios) EUR 12.5M at 30 Jun 2025, described as the highest in Liga 2; improvement vs -4.4M in 2023/24 credited to supplier cost cuts, adjusting the club to 'as verbas e o financiamento da II Liga', and transfer income that 'mitigated' the result.
- Club Sport Maritimo (club entity, separate from SAD): 2023/24 accounts approved with EUR 4.8M loss (club universe including equity-method effect of the SAD); 2024/25 club standalone result positive EUR 398k (Record headline slug says 388k) vs a EUR 608k loss the year before, while the consolidated club universe remained negative.
- Liga 2 club budget benchmark (Sportinforma, 'Orcamentos nao ditam resultados', season ~2021/22): the 16 Liga 2 clubs had combined budgets of ~EUR 22.5M, i.e. ~EUR 1.4M average per club, with only four clubs above EUR 2M; the I Liga average was EUR 18.7M/club (~EUR 5M excluding Benfica/Porto/Sporting) - so a 'small/mid' Liga 2 budget is roughly EUR 1-1.5M and a 'big' Liga 2 budget is EUR 2-4M+ (Maritimo, Torreense at ~8M opex are outliers at the top).
- Liga 2 sector P&L averages from the Liga Portugal/EY annual report for 2022/23 (cross-checks the figures you already hold): average revenue EUR 2.6M vs average cost EUR 4.0M per club; total Liga 2 costs EUR 63M (+0.4% YoY); total liabilities at an all-time high of EUR 97M (+24% YoY; average EUR 6.05M per club); total assets down 7% to EUR 45M (average EUR 2.781M per club); revenues fell 10% vs 2021/22; average stadium occupancy just 27%.
- Liga 2 update for 2023/24 (Liga Portugal/EY via DN, zerozero, Observador, Mar 2025): the 34 I+II Liga clubs had record combined revenue of EUR 1,073M, of which EUR 1,024M came from the 18 I Liga clubs - leaving roughly EUR 49M total for Liga 2, i.e. ~EUR 2.7M average per club; combined costs were EUR 1,122M (EUR 98M aggregate deficit); Liga 2 average attendance stayed below 2,000 per match.
- Small-club calibration points: Belenenses won promotion from Liga 3 on a ~EUR 650k budget (2022/23) and faced >EUR 500k of stadium-licensing/lighting costs just to enter Liga 2 in 2023/24 (president Patrick Morais de Carvalho, Observador May 2023); Leixoes SAD ran a balanced EUR 1.95M Liga 2 budget in 2017/18 (projected revenues EUR 1,955,328 vs expenses EUR 1,949,621); Varzim SDUQ (Liga 3 after relegation from Liga 2) lost EUR 1.05M in 2023/24 and EUR 820k in 2024/25 (club entity +440k and +372k respectively; combined 2024/25 universe -448k), with ~EUR 200k municipal support and EUR 70k FPF support receivable.
- CURRENT Liga 2 TV income (2024/25-2026/27): no per-club figure is public - rights are sold individually and values are confidential; Maritimo's president Carlos Andre Gomes explicitly refused to disclose the value of the club's Sport TV renewal in March 2026, saying there were 'no pre-determined values' and it depended on 'what the market is willing to pay'; since 2018/19 ALL Liga 2 matches are broadcast by Sport TV (8 per round on linear TV in recent seasons, ~6 more per round streamed free on Sport TV's site; B-team home games on Benfica TV/Porto Canal), which caps the matchday-TV value of an individual small club at a low level.
- Best documented per-club Liga 2 TV anchors: under the 2016 centralized MEO deal each Liga 2 club received EUR 1.5M over 3 seasons = EUR 500k per season (2016/17-2018/19, 19 clubs accepted; Observador/TSF), and the league's continuity proposal afterwards was EUR 300k per club per season; in that era promotion to Liga 1 roughly tripled a club's TV money to ~EUR 3.5M - current individual Sport TV deals for ordinary Liga 2 clubs are believed to be well below the 500k MEO level but no audited public number exists (see caveats).
- What a Liga 2 club verifiably receives centrally today: the UEFA solidarity mechanism distributed via Liga Portugal/FPF paid a record ~EUR 6.1M total to Liga 2 in 2024/25, i.e. ~EUR 382,400 per club for each of the 16 non-B-team clubs (A Bola 28 Feb 2025, RTP, Record) - for many small Liga 2 clubs this solidarity payment, not TV, is the main 'central' income line; in COVID-era 2019/20 the comparable support was up to EUR 180k per club (FPF EUR 62.5k + Liga funds); Liga Portugal's own 2025/26 budget (record EUR 31.8M revenues) distributes ~EUR 10M across I+II Liga sports societies.
- From 2028/29 (mandatory centralization, Decree-Law 22-B/2021; distribution key approved in AG on 8 June 2026 with ~80-90% of votes, Benfica against): Liga 2 collectively receives 10% of the centralized pool; at the Liga's EUR 225M reference valuation that is EUR 22.5M/season, i.e. ~EUR 1.25M average per Liga 2 club (18 clubs) - a step change vs today; total-pool scenarios run EUR 200-250M with a special mechanism up to a EUR 275M cap (half of any excess above 250M to the three biggest UEFA-ranking contributors).
- I Liga distribution key for promotion modeling (A Bola 'O dinheiro da TV' exercise, Apr 2026, on a EUR 202.5M I Liga pool): 33.2% equal shares to all 18 clubs, 44.2% sporting merit distributed 1-to-18 shares (unit ~EUR 523k; champion ~EUR 9.4M from the merit component alone), 17.6% attendances + TV audiences, 3% conditions offered for transmissions, 1% pitch/lighting/media infrastructure; the same 1-to-N share logic guarantees the bottom club a minimum TV revenue.
- Current I Liga contrast (why promotion matters): 2024/25 estimates put Benfica at ~EUR 40M and Sporting and FC Porto at ~EUR 35M each from individually sold rights (Sport TV/BTV/Media Capital), while a small I Liga club like Arouca gets ~EUR 3.5M/yr; a UEFA benchmarking study found Portuguese top-flight clubs average ~EUR 7M/yr domestic TV with a big-to-mid ratio above 1,500% (vs 240% average in centralized leagues) - Portugal is the last major European league selling individually (Sport TV held 17 of 18 I Liga clubs to end-2025/26).

## Primeira Liga (Liga Portugal Betclic) per-club payrolls/budgets 2025/26 and central revenues: individual TV deals, the 2028/29 centralization model, and league/UEFA distributions incl. relegation support

- Complete 2025/26 Primeira Liga payroll table exists via the Sportingpedia study (based on Capology first-team base wages, reported by Record 13 Oct 2025 and A Bola 8 Dec 2025): league total EUR 213.985M across 18 clubs; Benfica EUR 42.48M (19.85%), Sporting EUR 33.18M (15.51%, reported as 32.7M in the Oct version), FC Porto EUR 30.91M (14.44%), SC Braga EUR 16.5M (7.71%), Rio Ave EUR 9.69M (4.53%), Estoril EUR 9.51M (4.44%), Casa Pia EUR 8.39M (3.92%), Famalicao EUR 7.71M (3.60%), Arouca EUR 6.45M, Nacional EUR 6.435M (10th), Vitoria de Guimaraes EUR 6.17M (12th), Moreirense EUR 6.05M (13th), Gil Vicente EUR 5.72M (15th), AVS EUR 5.31M (16th), Tondela EUR 3.86M (1.80%), Alverca EUR 3.37M (1.57%, lowest); Santa Clara (11th, approx EUR 6.2-6.4M) and Estrela da Amadora (14th, approx EUR 5.7-6.0M) sit in the reported 5.31-6.45M band but exact values were not retrievable.
- The tier a promoted club joins: 10 of 18 Primeira Liga clubs spend under EUR 7M/year on first-team wages in 2025/26 (Arouca, Nacional, Santa Clara, V. Guimaraes, Moreirense, Estrela da Amadora, Gil Vicente, AVS, Tondela, Alverca), and Benfica outspends bottom club Alverca 12.6x (gap EUR 39.11M).
- Reported full-club budgets (orcamento) for bottom-half clubs 2025/26: Gil Vicente club EUR 8.18M plus SDUQ (professional football company) EUR 9.7293M approved June 2025 (rising to approx EUR 16M for 2026/27, biggest ever, +6M YoY); Arouca approved approx EUR 10M for 2025/26; these sit well above their wage bills and are the best proxy for what a small I Liga club spends in total.
- Individual TV rights today (no centralization until 2028/29; Sport TV holds 17 of the 18 clubs' rights through 2025/26, Benfica being the NOS outlier): A Bola's April 2026 exercise estimates end-2024/25 TV receipts of approx EUR 40M for Benfica, approx EUR 35M each for Sporting and FC Porto, while typical small I Liga clubs (Gil Vicente, Arouca, Casa Pia cited) receive approx EUR 3.5M per season; the whole Portuguese TV market is worth approx EUR 180M/year with the big three concentrating 61% (approx EUR 110M).
- Benfica-NOS: the legacy 2015 deal was EUR 400M over 10 years (approx EUR 40M/season, 75% sporting rights / 25% BTV exclusivity); on 26 Jan 2026 Benfica SAD signed a 2-season extension with NOS for 2026/27-2027/28 worth EUR 104.6M (EUR 52.3M/season) for home-match TV/multimedia rights, plus EUR 2.4M BTV advertising and EUR 7.2M stadium dynamic advertising, totalling EUR 114.2M over the two seasons (approx EUR 57.1M/year all-in) - the largest TV contract ever in Portugal.
- FC Porto's deal with Altice/PT (MEO) was a EUR 457.5M global package (signed 2015, running to 2027/28) and Sporting's with NOS a EUR 515M package covering TV rights, shirt sponsorship, stadium advertising and Sporting TV exclusivity - i.e., the headline totals are not pure TV money; current annual TV value for each is estimated at approx EUR 35M (A Bola, Apr 2026).
- Benchmark for a promoted small club's TV income: the 2016 MEO centralized Liga 2 deal paid each II Liga club EUR 500k/season and tripled to EUR 3.5M/season upon promotion to the I Liga - consistent with the approx EUR 3.5M small-club figure still reported for 2024/25-2025/26.
- Centralization becomes mandatory from 2028/29 (2021 decree-law): Liga Portugal's reference valuation is EUR 225M/season gross (ceiling reference EUR 250M; the earlier EUR 300M ambition was abandoned per Publico, Oct 2025), split 90% to the I Liga and 10% to Liga 2.
- The approved I Liga distribution key (AG of 8 June 2026, approx 80% majority): 33.2% equal shares to all 18 clubs, 44.2% sporting merit (final league position, historical classifications, contribution to UEFA ranking), 17.6% stadium attendances and TV audiences, 3% transmission conditions, 1% pitch/lighting/media infrastructure; an earlier Aug 2025 draft used 32.5% equal / 44.2% merit / 14.3% 'grandeza e implantacao social' / 5% infrastructure / 4% transmission.
- Derived per-club floor under centralization (calculation, not a reported figure): at EUR 225M gross, the I Liga pool is EUR 202.5M and the 33.2% equal-share tranche gives every I Liga club approx EUR 3.74M guaranteed before merit/audience components.
- A Bola's April 2026 simulation at EUR 225M: Benfica would lose EUR 19.4M vs current TV income, FC Porto EUR 16.6M, Sporting EUR 15.8M (big three capped at approx 28% of the total, approx EUR 20M each), while small clubs roughly double or triple: Arouca EUR 3.5M to 7.1M, Casa Pia EUR 3.5M to 9.9M, Gil Vicente EUR 3.5M to approx 11M; an ECO interview estimated Benfica could lose up to EUR 15M/year.
- No explicit parachute payment for relegation from the I Liga exists today, and no parachute mechanic has been publicly defined in the approved 2028/29 distribution key as of Aug 2026; the merit criterion's 'historical classifications' component and the Liga 2's 10% share are the only cushioning elements identified.
- De facto relegation support today comes from UEFA solidarity money routed via the FPF: for 2024/25 the 16 Liga 2 SADs (excluding B teams) received EUR 6.119M, i.e. EUR 382,437 each (Feb 2025, record at the time), and Liga 2 'Meu Super' clubs later received a record verba estimated above EUR 6M via FPF-Liga articulation; in 2023/24 the FPF split EUR 6.662M across 30 clubs of both leagues at EUR 222k each.
- For 2025/26 the Liga general assembly (Feb 2026) rejected extending the UEFA solidarity mechanism to Liga 2, removing approx EUR 6M that second-tier clubs had already budgeted - Liga 2 clubs publicly protested a 'grave financial imbalance'; the pool referenced was EUR 12M.
- UEFA solidarity to non-European I Liga clubs: 2024/25 the FPF distributed EUR 7.519M equally among 13 I Liga clubs outside Europe (EUR 578,384.62 each, July 2025); for 2025/26 it distributes EUR 8.5M among 14 clubs (approx EUR 607k each, largest ever, +EUR 1M YoY), excluding Benfica, Sporting (UCL), Porto, Braga (UEL); Santa Clara qualifies despite its Conference League qualifier elimination.
- Actual UEFA competition receipts 2024/25 (UEFA distribution report via O Jogo/Maisfutebol/TVI, Feb 2026): Benfica EUR 71.434M (incl. EUR 24.542M coefficient bonus; UCL quarter-finals), Sporting EUR 48.983M (incl. EUR 17.548M coefficient), FC Porto EUR 16.488M (UEL), Braga EUR 13.437M (UEL league phase), Vitoria de Guimaraes EUR 9.404M (Conference League round of 16).
- Liga Portugal (LPFP) own budget 2025/26: record revenues of EUR 31.8M, allowing distribution of approx EUR 10M in dividends across the sports companies of both professional leagues (approved June 2025); operating result forecast EUR 580k.
- Context for 2026/27 planning: Tondela and AVS were relegated from the 2025/26 Primeira Liga; Alverca finished 11th, Nacional 14th, Estrela da Amadora 15th - so the promoted-club survival tier continues to operate on EUR 3.4-6.5M wage bills and EUR 8-10M total budgets.

**Caveats:** Methodology caveats: (1) The network egress proxy blocked direct WebFetch of nearly all source pages (record.pt, abola.pt, sportingpedia.com, capology.com, sapo.pt, dnoticias.pt, fbref.com), so all figures were extracted from search-result content of the cited articles and cross-checked across at least two outlets where possible; exact wording of some tables could not be verified against the original page. (2) The 2025/26 payroll table is the Sportingpedia study built on Capology data, i.e. ESTIMATED first-team gross base wages, not audited accounts - FC Porto's own accounts imply a much higher total wage bill (approx EUR 50.7M incl. bonuses per one source), and Benfica/Sporting/Porto annual reports (Relatorio e Contas) show total staff costs well above these player-only figures; treat the table as internally consistent for cross-club comparison, not as statutory payroll. (3) Santa Clara (11th) and Estrela da Amadora (14th) exact values were not retrievable; the bands given are inferred from the study's published ordering and the 5.31-6.45M bracket - flag as estimates. Sporting appears as 32.7M (Record, Oct 2025) and 33.18M (A Bola/Sportingpedia, Dec 2025); Braga appeared once as 21.2M (aggregator) vs 16.5M in the Dec study - the Dec study values are used. (4) Budget (orcamento) figures are only publicly approved for member-owned clubs (Gil Vicente, Arouca found); most SAD-run small clubs do not publish season budgets, so payroll is the only consistent 18-club metric. The Santa Clara EUR 3.5M budget article could not be dated (likely the 2018/19 promotion season, not 2024/25 or 2025/26). Benfica EUR 115M (already in project) was not re-verified this session. (5) Current per-club TV values (Benfica approx 40M, Sporting/Porto approx 35M, small clubs approx 3.5M, market approx 180M) are A Bola/Sportinforma journalistic estimates, not contract disclosures - only the Benfica-NOS deals (400M/10yr; 104.6M/2yr signed Jan 2026) and the historical Porto 457.5M / Sporting 515M package totals are reported contract values, and the latter two bundle non-TV items. (6) All post-centralization per-club numbers (Benfica -19.4M, Gil Vicente to approx 11M, equal-share floor approx 3.74M) are simulations/calculations at the EUR 225M reference value, which is itself a Liga valuation, not a signed contract; Benfica was still contesting the process (voted against, sought suspension) as of mid-2026, so parameters may change. (8) UEFA solidarity distributions changed year to year (222k/club 2023/24 both leagues; 382k/578k split 2024/25; I Liga-only approx 607k in 2025/26 after the Feb 2026 vote) - do not extrapolate a single parachute-like figure forward.

## Liga Portugal Betclic (Primeira Liga) club budgets: big-three revenues, mid-tier and small-club budgets, TV rights (current individual deals and 2028/29 centralization), wage bills, promoted-club economics, UEFA money, and league distribution

- Big three revenues 2024/25 (Relatorio e Contas): Benfica consolidated operating revenues ex-player-sales were EUR 284m (+26% YoY; EUR 411.5m including player transactions; TV+competition prizes EUR 148.4m, matchday EUR 62.8m, commercial EUR 72.8m); Sporting posted record volume de negocios of EUR 265m with operating revenues ex-player-transactions of EUR 148.1m (+45%); FC Porto had operating revenues ex-player-transactions of EUR 149.5m (down from EUR 174.5m in 2023/24 due to no Champions League) plus EUR 171m in player-sale revenues.
- Net results 2024/25: Benfica profit EUR 40.7m consolidated (SAD figure EUR 34.4m), FC Porto record profit EUR 39.2m, Sporting profit EUR 20m (+66% vs EUR 12.1m in 2023/24), i.e. all three big SADs were profitable.
- Wage bills (custos com pessoal) 2024/25: Benfica EUR 127.7m (vs 114.9m in 2023/24, inflated by EUR 10.1m payoff to Roger Schmidt's staff), Sporting EUR 87.7m (down from 90.3m), FC Porto EUR 81.9m (down 11% from 92.4m); by comparison SC Braga paid EUR 24.5m in salaries (~EUR 39.5m personnel costs for the whole structure) - so Benfica's wage bill alone is roughly 15-25x a small club's entire budget.
- Mid-tier 2024/25: SC Braga had operating income ex-player-rights of EUR 34.5m, global income of EUR 69.75m, operating costs ex-player-ops of EUR 62.0m and a net LOSS of EUR 11.0m; Vitoria SC SAD posted its best-ever profit of EUR 7.61m driven by EUR 27.5m in player-sale capital gains and EUR 9.7m+ in UEFA Conference League money; Famalicao SAD (2023/24, latest confirmed) had EBITDA of EUR 14m and net profit of EUR 8m.
- Small-club budgets (press-reported orcamentos): Arouca projected EUR 7.5m for 2024/25 and approved EUR 10.4m for 2025/26; Casa Pia ~EUR 5m and Estoril ~EUR 5.5m (2024/25); Tondela historically the league's lowest budget (EUR 3m in 2021/22, again lowest in 2025/26); Moreirense's actual 2024/25 accounts showed revenues of EUR 14.57m (vs 6.81m in 2023/24) and expenses of EUR 9.2m with a record EUR 5.4m profit. Typical small-club operating budget is EUR 5-10m, i.e. ~2-4% of Benfica's revenue.
- Newly promoted club economics: Alverca (promoted for 2025/26) said its fixed revenues are ~EUR 5m and that it 'starts the season losing EUR 5m', building a squad from scratch with 26 signings; a promoted club's guaranteed TV money under current individual deals is only ~EUR 3-3.5m/season; no public budget figures exist for AVS or Nacional's first seasons (2024/25). Estrela da Amadora shows the downside risk: by May 2026 its debt exceeded EUR 25m and it asked creditors to forgive 90% (EUR 20m) to avoid exclusion from the Liga, anticipating only ~EUR 14m of revenues over coming seasons.
- Current TV rights (individual deals with Sport TV via NOS/MEO wholesale, running until 2027/28): the top-earning club receives ~EUR 36m/season while the bottom clubs get ~EUR 3-3.5m - a 12x spread that UEFA has called the worst TV-revenue distribution in Europe. Small clubs like Gil Vicente, Arouca and Casa Pia each receive ~EUR 3.5m/season guaranteed.
- Big-three TV contracts: Benfica signed with NOS in January 2026 for 2026/27+2027/28 at EUR 104.6m for TV rights proper (EUR 52.3m/season; EUR 114.2m or 57.1m/season including BTV advertising EUR 2.4m and stadium dynamic advertising EUR 7.2m) - the highest TV contract ever in Portugal; FC Porto's long-term MEO/Altice deal totals EUR 457m and Sporting's NOS deal totals EUR 446m in progressive annual amounts (both multi-year deals signed around 2015 running to ~2027/28, averaging roughly EUR 35-40m/season); Braga sold to NOS for EUR 100m/10 years (~EUR 10m/season) and Vitoria SC to MEO for EUR 90m/10 years (~EUR 8m/season, later reported as a EUR 200m/10-year package including stadium naming).
- Centralized TV rights from 2028/29 (mandatory under decreto-lei 22-B/2021; Liga Portugal's distribution proposal approved April 2026): base scenario EUR 225m gross per season (scenarios up to EUR 250-275m defined, one report cites potential up to EUR 325m), split 90% to Liga Betclic and 10% to Liga 2.
- Centralization distribution key for the I Liga: 33.2% shared equally among all 18 clubs; 44.2% by sporting merit (final position on an 18:1 champion-to-last ladder where each step is worth ~EUR 523k, plus historical classification and UEFA-ranking contribution); in the EUR 225m scenario the champion's merit share is ~EUR 9.4m and the last-placed ~EUR 0.5m; if proceeds exceed EUR 250m, half the excess goes to the three clubs contributing most UEFA ranking points.
- Modeled per-club impact of centralization at EUR 225m (A Bola exercise, April 2026): Gil Vicente would go from EUR 3.5m to ~EUR 11m/season, Casa Pia from 3.5m to ~9.9m, Arouca from 3.5m to ~7.1m - i.e. small clubs roughly triple their guaranteed TV income, while the big three earn less than their individual deals.
- UEFA competition money actually received in 2024/25: Benfica EUR 71.434m (Champions League quarter-finals, incl. EUR 24.542m coefficient bonus), Sporting EUR 48.983m (UCL knockout play-off, EUR 17.548m coefficient bonus), FC Porto EUR 16.488m (Europa League play-off), Braga EUR 13.437m (UEL league phase), Vitoria SC EUR 9.404m (Conference League round of 16); for 2025/26 Sporting, Benfica and Porto had EUR 156m combined guaranteed from UEFA, and winning the Portuguese title is worth ~EUR 40m immediately via direct UCL league-phase entry.
- UEFA solidarity for non-participants: the FPF distributed EUR 8.5m of UEFA solidarity money across 14 Liga Betclic clubs not in European competition (~EUR 0.6m per club) - a meaningful sum relative to EUR 5m small-club budgets.
- Domestic league distribution is small: Liga Portugal's own budget was EUR 31.5m in 2024/25 (EUR 9.8m distributed to member SADs of both divisions) and a record EUR 31.8m in 2025/26 (~EUR 10m distributed, of which only ~EUR 2.8m as direct prizes, the rest in-kind via refereeing, delegates and balls); there is no large per-position domestic prize pot today - the financial reward of league position comes almost entirely from TV deals and UEFA qualification until centralization starts in 2028/29.
- Scale summary for a budget analyst: Primeira Liga club budgets span roughly EUR 3-5m (promoted/smallest clubs) through EUR 5-10m (established small clubs), EUR 15-70m gross income (mid-tier Braga/Vitoria/Famalicao/Moreirense with player trading), to EUR 148-284m core revenues at the big three - Portuguese professional football's aggregate revenue passed EUR 1bn for the first time in 2023/24.

## Football salaries in Portugal by tier (players and coaches): CCT minimum wages, reported averages, wage bills, employer cost loadings and bonus structures for Primeira Liga, Liga Portugal 2, Liga 3 and Campeonato de Portugal

- The CCT (collective agreement) between Liga Portugal and the players union SJPF sets minimum monthly player wages for 2025/26 as multiples of the national minimum wage (RMMG, EUR 870/month in 2025): Primeira Liga (Liga Portugal Betclic) 3x RMMG = EUR 2,610; Liga Portugal 2 1.75x RMMG = EUR 1,522.50; Liga 3 1.5x RMMG = EUR 1,305; Campeonato de Portugal, Liga Revelacao, youth and all other competitions 1x RMMG = EUR 870 (source: Sindicato dos Jogadores, 'Salarios minimos para 2025/2026').
- Escape clauses lower the effective floors in the lower tiers: Liga 3 clubs may pay only the RMMG (EUR 870) if at least 50% of the first squad hold sports employment contracts registered with the FPF, and Campeonato de Portugal clubs may cut contracts to the RMMG if at least 30% of the squad hold registered professional contracts (sources: Sindicato dos Jogadores 2025/26 note; Maisfutebol).
- Total Primeira Liga player wage spend for 2025/26 is EUR 213.985M across 18 clubs, i.e. about EUR 11.9M average squad wage bill per club, with an extreme spread: Benfica EUR 42.48M (19.85% of the league total), Sporting EUR 32.7M, FC Porto EUR 30.91M, and promoted Alverca lowest at roughly EUR 3.4M (Benfica pays 12x more; gap EUR 39.11M) (source: Sportingpedia study, Oct 2025, reported by Record, Observador and A Bola).
- Reported average Primeira Liga player pay 2025/26: around EUR 130,000 gross per year league-wide (about EUR 9,300/month on 14 payments) per SalaryLeaks, jumping to about EUR 850,000/year average at the top four clubs; note the simple mean implied by EUR 214M over ~490 players is ~EUR 435k/year, so the EUR 130k figure behaves like a median — treat the distribution as heavily right-skewed.
- Highest individual Primeira Liga salaries 2025/26: Francisco Trincao (Sporting) EUR 4.49M/year gross and Anatoliy Trubin (Benfica) EUR 4.43M/year; FootyStats/Capology estimate average pay of EUR 291,682/year for players aged 18-23 and EUR 565,970/year for players over 30 (estimates, known-salary sample only).
- Audited 2024/25 total personnel costs (players + technical staff + administration, from the SADs' Relatorio e Contas): Sporting SAD EUR 87.7M (down from 90.3M), FC Porto SAD EUR 81.9M (down 11%), Benfica SAD clearly above both, its year-on-year rise of EUR 12.8M largely explained by EUR 10.1M paid out to Roger Schmidt's dismissed technical team (sources: CNN Portugal/Maisfutebol from club accounts).
- Liga Portugal 2 aggregate finances (2022/23, Liga Portugal data via Maisfutebol): total club revenues EUR 42M vs total costs EUR 63M, i.e. average revenue EUR 2.6M and average total costs EUR 4.0M per club; with wages typically 50-70% of costs this implies average squad wage bills of roughly EUR 2.0-2.8M per Liga 2 club (implied estimate, not a reported figure).
- No published average salary exists for Liga Portugal 2; the negotiated floor is EUR 1,522.50/month (2025/26) and mid-tier squad economics (EUR 2-2.8M implied wage bill over 25-30 players) imply average player pay on the order of EUR 3,000-6,000/month gross (estimate).
- Liga 3 is formally semi-professional; no public average salary exists — the CCT floor is EUR 1,305/month (or EUR 870 under the 50%-contracts rule), and with 25-30 player squads a floor-level squad costs roughly EUR 0.35-0.55M/year in gross wages (25-28 players x EUR 1,000-1,400 x 14 payments), with competitive promotion-chasing Liga 3 squads plausibly EUR 0.6-1.0M (implied estimate).
- Campeonato de Portugal (tier 4) players earn from EUR 870/month (2025/26 CCT floor) with many squads mixing amateurs and expense-allowance players; wage arrears are documented (e.g. 1.º Dezembro players unpaid for 3 months, Record 2024), so budget for floor-level wages of EUR 0.1-0.3M/year per squad (implied estimate).
- Coach minimum pay is set by the separate LPFP-ANTF coaches' CCT as multiples of the national minimum wage: head coach Primeira Liga 8x RMMG (EUR 6,960/month at 2025 RMMG), second tier 4x (EUR 3,480), old II Divisao B (today's tier-3 equivalent) 3x (EUR 2,610), old III Divisao 2x (EUR 1,740), other divisions/youth 1x (EUR 870); assistant coaches are guaranteed at least 50% of the head-coach minimum, and coaches also receive holiday and Christmas subsidies each equal to one month's base pay (source: CCT LPFP-ANTF).
- Actual top-flight head-coach pay is far above the CCT floor: Benfica's coach around EUR 3M/year, and Sergio Conceicao at FC Porto was reported to earn about double Roger Schmidt (~EUR 6-7M vs ~EUR 3-3.5M, 2023/24 reporting by Futebol Divertido/Bola na Rede); mid- and small Primeira Liga clubs are commonly reported in the EUR 200k-600k/year range and Liga 2 head coaches roughly EUR 3,500-10,000/month (estimates — no audited per-coach data).
- Portuguese salaries are paid 14 times per year: 12 monthly wages plus a holiday subsidy (subsidio de ferias) and a Christmas subsidy (subsidio de Natal), each equal to one month's base pay — both the players' and coaches' CCTs restate this, so annual gross = monthly wage x 14 (sources: CGD Saldo Positivo; CCTs).
- Standard employer social security (TSU) in Portugal is 23.75% of gross pay (employee pays 11%) — this applies to coaches and general staff; but professional sportspeople under a sports employment contract have a special regime in the Codigo dos Regimes Contributivos: total rate 33.3% (22.3% employer / 11% player) applied to a contribution base of only ONE FIFTH of actual remuneration (minimum base 1x IAS, EUR 522.50 in 2025), giving an effective employer cost of about 4.46% of gross player pay unless the parties opt in writing for the full-pay base (sources: Pluxee/MMC for TSU; Caldeira Pires technical note and CRC arts. 70-71 for athletes' regime).
- Practical employer-cost formula per player: annual employer cost = monthly gross x 14 x ~1.045 (special athletes' TSU on 1/5 base) — e.g. a Liga 3 floor player at EUR 1,305/month costs ~EUR 19,100/year; per coach/staff member: monthly gross x 14 x 1.2375 — e.g. a Liga 3 coach at EUR 2,610/month costs ~EUR 45,200/year.
- Bonus structures ('premios de jogo') are negotiated club by club and are recognised but not priced in the CCT: at the top end Sporting and Benfica each budgeted about EUR 5M in squad bonus pools for winning the 2024/25 title (JN), Champions League clubs pass through part of UEFA's EUR 2.1M-per-win prize, and the Taca de Portugal pays the winning club EUR 325k and runner-up EUR 175k (2025/26, record-high pot of EUR 2.322M); win/point bonuses at Liga 3/CP level are typically small per-match cash amounts and promotion bonuses (premio de subida) of roughly one extra month's salary to several months are customary but no public figures exist (estimate).
- The league itself pays no prize money for winning the Primeira Liga; Liga Portugal's own budget distributes about EUR 10M to member clubs in 2025/26 (record EUR 23.5M+ league budgets) rising to over EUR 11M in 2026/27, so essentially all performance pay at club level is club-funded or UEFA/FPF-funded (sources: Record; Maisfutebol).
- Squad size norms for budgeting: professional Portuguese squads run 25-30 players (Primeira Liga ~27-30 including squad-listed youth; Liga 2 ~25-28; Liga 3 ~23-27 with sub-23 quota rules in the FPF regulation; CP squads often larger but partly amateur); the SJPF/Liga have also introduced squad registration limits ('Limite de registos e composicao dos planteis') — exact caps are in the FPF Liga 3 regulation PDF (fpf.pt, blocked from direct fetch).

**Caveats:** 1) Real reported figures vs estimates: CCT minimums, TSU rates, 14-payment rule, Primeira Liga wage bills (Sportingpedia/Record), 2024/25 audited personnel costs, Liga 2 aggregate finances and Taca prize money are reported figures. The following are ESTIMATES flagged as such: Liga 2 average player pay (EUR 3,000-6,000/month), Liga 3 typical pay range and squad wage bills, Campeonato de Portugal squad wage bills, non-big-3 coach salaries, and lower-league win/promotion bonus norms — no public data exists for these; media only confirm the CCT floors. 2) Sportingpedia (EUR 213.985M league total) covers player wages only, gross, and is itself an aggregation of estimates; Capology figures are model estimates on a known-salary sample; FC Porto shows EUR 30.91M (Sportingpedia) vs EUR 50.7M incl. bonuses (SalaryLeaks) — treat per-club figures as +/-30%. The EUR 130k 'average' (SalaryLeaks) is inconsistent with the arithmetic mean (~EUR 435k) implied by the league total; it likely reflects a median. 3) RMMG timing: the 2025/26 CCT minimums were set against the 2025 RMMG of EUR 870; one source claimed RMMG stays EUR 870 in 2026 while the tripartite agreement targets EUR 920 — if EUR 920 applies from Jan 2026, floors rise proportionally (e.g. Primeira Liga EUR 2,760). 4) The coaches' CCT tier multiples (8x/4x/3x/2x/1x) come from the LPFP-ANTF agreement whose tier names predate Liga 3/CP (II Divisao B, III Divisao); applying 3x to Liga 3 and 2x to CP is an interpretation. Direct PDF fetch was blocked (antf.pt, ligaportugalstorage, fpf.pt), values verified only via search snippets. 5) Several key Portuguese domains were egress-blocked (sjogadores.pt, record.pt, observador.pt, rtp.pt, sapo.pt, iol.pt group, abola.pt indirectly, fpf.pt, antf.pt, patriciacoelho.pt, bolanarede.pt), so numbers rely on search-result summaries cross-checked across 2+ independent result sets where possible. 6) The special athletes' social-security base (1/5 of pay, ~4.46% effective employer cost) applies only to players on a registered contrato de trabalho desportivo; coaches and other staff carry the full 23.75% TSU; clubs may also owe work-accident insurance (mandatory, typically 2-5% of payroll for athletes — not sourced here). 7) Audited 'gastos com pessoal' (Sporting EUR 87.7M etc.) include all employees, severance and bonuses, so they exceed squad-only wage bills. 8) Liga 2 aggregate finance data is 2022/23 — the most recent league-level disclosure found; expect modest inflation since.

## Non-TV revenue streams of lower-tier Portuguese football clubs (Liga 3 and Liga Portugal 2): matchday, sponsorship, municipal support, membership quotas, player trading, and revenue composition

- MATCHDAY: Liga 3 ticket prices cluster at EUR 5 per match (Varzim SC home games EUR 5 via SmartFan; Fafe x Varzim EUR 5; away sectors at SC Braga B EUR 7.50). Members with paid-up quotas often enter free or discounted, so matchday cash yield per attendee is low.
- ATTENDANCE: Liga 3 averaged 1,155 spectators/match in 2024/25 (341,944 total over 296 matches); Liga Portugal 2 averaged 1,818 spectators/match in 2023/24 (556,267 total). So a typical Liga 3 club's gate is roughly 1,000-1,200 x ~EUR 3-5 net per paying head x 17 home games - i.e. bilheteira is usually well under EUR 100k/season for a Liga 3 club.
- MUNICIPAL SUPPORT is a major, well-documented revenue line: Camara Municipal da Povoa de Varzim's contrato-programa to Varzim SC (youth/formacao) rose from EUR 300k (2022) to EUR 315k (2023), EUR 400k (2024) and EUR 450k (2025). Povoa de Varzim distributes ~EUR 2.2-2.3M/year across ~25 local associations (other big recipients: Clube Desportivo da Povoa EUR 363.5k, Povoa Andebol EUR 252.5k). Comparable municipal envelopes: Castelo Branco EUR 607.5k for all football/futsal clubs (2026/27 season), Coimbra EUR 305k across 51 entities, small clubs like Clube Olimpico do Montijo EUR 18.4k (2023/24). Note: municipal support can be conditional - Povoa suspended support to a club that sued the municipality (2025).
- MEMBERSHIP (quotas de socios): typical lower-tier quota is EUR 2.50-10/month. CF Os Belenenses (Liga 3): adults EUR 10/month (EUR 120/yr), youth EUR 2.50/month, distant members EUR 40/yr. Desportivo de Chaves kept its annual quota at EUR 50 after relegation to Liga 2 (2025). Varzim SC had 1,757 members with quotas up to date in 2021/22 (target 2,600) and claimed >5,000 registered members in Sept 2023 - so realistic quota income for a Varzim-sized club is roughly EUR 90k-150k/yr if ~2,000-2,500 members pay ~EUR 50-60/yr.
- SPONSORSHIP: no public per-club shirt-sponsor figures exist for Liga 2/Liga 3 (deals undisclosed; media coverage covers only Benfica/Porto/Sporting). Sponsorship at this level is mostly local SMEs (stadium boards, shirt logos, in-kind). Varzim's structure splits commercial rights: the SAD/SDUQ keeps in-stadium advertising, ticketing, bar and merchandising; the club keeps outside-stadium advertising and events. Treat shirt+total sponsorship for a Liga 3 club as low tens to low hundreds of thousands EUR - not directly sourced, must be estimated.
- PLAYER TRADING: the standard mechanisms are well quantified even where individual fees are not. FIFA training compensation (UEFA rates): Category III clubs EUR 30,000 per training year, Category IV EUR 10,000 per training year (most Portuguese Liga 2 clubs are Cat III, lower-tier Cat IV; ages 12-15 always billed at Cat IV rates). Solidarity mechanism: 5% of any international transfer fee split across clubs that trained the player aged 12-23 (0.25% of the fee per season aged 12-15, 0.5% per season aged 16-23). FPF distributed EUR 7.5M of UEFA solidarity money to non-top-flight Portuguese clubs. Sell-on clauses (percentage of future sale) are standard practice in Portuguese deals. Concrete lower-tier fee disclosures are rare - Liga 3 players moving between clubs typically go free after contract expiry (e.g. Miguel Pereira, Felgueiras to Lusitania Lourosa, free).
- TOTAL REVENUE SCALE / COMPOSITION: Belenenses won promotion from Liga 3 in 2022/23 on a budget of ~EUR 650k while rivals had 'triple that' - so Liga 3 budgets span roughly EUR 0.5-2M. Varzim SC (club side) budgeted ~EUR 746k revenues for a season, with the SDUQ (professional football) posting a EUR -1.1M result in 2021/22 and the club EUR -12k. Liga 2 aggregate: the 16 non-B-team II Liga clubs shared roughly EUR 49M of the record EUR 1,073M total professional-football revenue in 2023/24 (I Liga took EUR 1,024M), i.e. ~EUR 3M average revenue per Liga 2 club. Liga Portugal licensing/stadium requirements alone cost clubs over EUR 500k when moving up to II Liga. One-off non-football income matters at this level: Academica made ~EUR 93k profit from managing Estadio Cidade de Coimbra (2022/23) and received EUR 300k from a Coldplay concert promoter; Varzim members approved selling the old training ground and headquarters for a minimum EUR 2.571M to pay debts (2024).

**Caveats:** Figures come from web-search summaries, not full page fetches (several key domains egress-blocked), so numbers should be treated as reported-by-press rather than audited. Shirt-sponsor values for Liga 2/Liga 3 clubs are not publicly disclosed anywhere found - the sponsorship estimate (low tens to low hundreds of thousands EUR per small club) is inference, not a sourced figure. No concrete disclosed transfer-fee example for a Liga 3 club selling a player was found (fees at this level are almost never published; most moves are free transfers), so player-trading income should be modeled via training compensation (EUR 10k-30k/training year) and the 5% solidarity mechanism. Portugal's exact FIFA club categorisation per tier was not confirmed from an FPF source (blocked); Cat III for Liga 2 and Cat IV for Liga 3 is the standard assumption. The ~EUR 3M average Liga 2 club revenue is derived (1,073M total minus 1,024M I Liga, over 16 clubs) and includes TV money, so the non-TV share is materially lower. Varzim SC figures mix the club (associacao) and its SDUQ - keep the entities separate when budgeting. Liga 2 attendance figure is 2023/24; 2024/25 league-wide average was not found. Some cited match-ticket pages date from the 2025/26 season (Varzim back in higher tiers after promotion), but the EUR 5 price level matches earlier Liga 3 seasons.
